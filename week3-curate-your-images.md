## 2. Where I chose a real capture over AI

- **Langganin screenshots** (3): these are the load-bearing proof of my whole claim ("I build products that get used"). No generated dashboard could carry that weight — a fake UI would collapse the argument the moment anyone looks at the live link. Real, uncropped, swipeable.
- **Profile photo**: the subject is me, and the audience is an HRD deciding whether to trust me. Honesty is the pitch. Generated faces were never an option.
- **Merpati / KulinerKita**: no real capture exists (privacy + non-visual project). I chose honest text cards rather than inventing a screenshot. This is the "knowing when a real screenshot beats anything generated" judgment in reverse — the absence is the honest call.

## 3. Rejected image — and why

**Rejected: "AI-generated dashboard mockup" for the Langganin case study.**

Early on, it was tempting to generate a clean, polished dashboard mockup — brighter, "perfect" data, no real-life mess, and it would save me the trouble of cropping real pages. I rejected it for a reason I can defend:

- It is a **lie**. The whole page's claim is that Langganin is live and you can open it yourself. A generated screenshot contradicts that in the same glance — one look at the real link and the fiction shows.
- The proof only works if it's **the actual product**. "I build things that get used" needs the real thing on the page, not an idealized drawing of it.
- A real screenshot, even with a half-filled state or honest empty areas, *builds trust*; a perfect generated image *costs it*.

So: real captures won. The only AI I would consider is connective tissue (a hero texture), and even that must match the identity kit's mood — and the hero already works without it, so I left it out rather than add decoration for its own sake.
